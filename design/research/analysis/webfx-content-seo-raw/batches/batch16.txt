What “SEO expert” actually means today
Before evaluating providers, it helps to align on what an “SEO expert” can look like in practice. An SEO expert can be a freelancer, in-house SEO professional, or an SEO agency. The right choice often depends on how much support you need and how your internal team is structured.
Below is a practical way to think about the three most common options:
Freelancers: Best for defined scopes of work
Freelancers can work well for defined scopes, audits, or tactical execution. They’re often effective when SEO needs are narrow and expectations are clearly set.
They tend to fall short as complexity increases. SEO often requires coordination across content, development, analytics, and other marketing efforts.
When the coordination work increases, a freelancer may be pulled in different directions and stretched thin, as they lack the bandwidth, tools, and cross-functional support.
In-house SEO: Best for organizations with large volumes of SEO work
An in-house SEO hire offers close alignment with internal teams and greater day-to-day control. This model can work well for organizations with the budget and workload to justify a full-time role focused on SEO.
For example, large ecommerce brands with thousands of product pages or media companies publishing high volumes of content often benefit from having in-house SEO teams. In these environments, SEO touches nearly every team (content, development, product, and analytics), making close internal collaboration especially valuable.
The tradeoffs include ramp time, limited coverage outside the individual’s core skill set, and reliance on a single perspective, especially as search algorithms, content formats, and reporting expectations evolve.
Agencies: Best for businesses that need access to various specialists
Agencies provide scale, established systems, and access to multiple specialists. They’re often better equipped to handle technical SEO, content strategy, analytics, and ongoing optimization in parallel as algorithms and the search landscape evolve.
The effectiveness of an agency partnership depends on process maturity, communication, and how well the agency aligns its work with your business goals. Strong agencies act as strategic partners, not just execution vendors.
How to evaluate SEO experts without getting misled
Most SEO agencies look qualified at first glance. The real differences show up when you ask the right questions and know what to look for before you sign a contract.
Use the steps below while you’re evaluating SEO experts (during sales calls, proposal reviews, and site research) to understand how they work and whether they’re set up to deliver results for your business. You can also download our checklist below!
Download our guide for evaluating SEO experts
1. Define your SEO goals
Before you talk to agencies, clearly define your SEO goals. What do you want your SEO efforts to achieve?
Rankings and traffic can be useful indicators, but they’re not goals in themselves. What matters is how SEO drives qualified leads, sales inquiries, or revenue growth over time.
Here’s what you can do:
Write down one to two business goals you want SEO to support: If you’re marketing SaaS for dental clinics, an example of a business goal is increasing demo requests from organic traffic.
Decide what progress would realistically look like in the first six to 12 months: Early progress might mean improved visibility for branded keywords or better lead quality.
Here’s what you can ask an SEO expert:
“How would you define success for a business like ours?” SEO professionals must be able to tie results to your business goals and not just rankings or traffic. They must consider sales inquiries and demo requests from organic search traffic, and even revenue influenced by organic traffic.
“Which metrics would you prioritize, and why?”: Be cautious if the answer focuses only on site traffic metrics. Look for explanations that connect metrics to decision-making and long-term growth.
Pro tip: Be cautious if an agency focuses only on rankings, total traffic, or impressions without explaining how those numbers support your business goals. Those metrics can be useful, but they don’t tell you whether SEO is actually working.
2. Determine a realistic budget aligned with your goals
Another thing businesses must clearly define before speaking with and evaluating an agency is their budget.
“The common best practice that shouldn’t necessarily be avoided, but reframed is budget,” Sarah B., Lead SEO Consultant at WebFX, points out. She shares that businesses typically set a budget, but the figure isn’t realistic given what they’re trying to accomplish.
She explains: “These budgets are either based on past spend (even though companies are hiring an agency because they’re unhappy with their current partner’s performance) or based on too-good-to-be-true pricing that’s promoted in ads and on websites.”
So how should businesses approach this? Sarah’s tip is to know your “number” for what would make your partnership with an SEO expert profitable. This number could be either in qualified leads or online sales.
Sarah explains: “For example, do you require 55 qualified leads per month or $25,000 in online sales to justify your SEO spending? Get aligned on those numbers when you’re hiring an SEO expert.”
3. Look for strategy, not just a list of tasks
Strong SEO experts can explain why certain work comes first, what will have the biggest impact, and what can wait. Their recommendations should feel specific to your business and not recycled from a template.
“In the vetting process, find out if they understand your industry,” advises Sarah. “It’s important that they speak the language of your industry and they must have the reviews and case studies to back their credibility up,” Sarah explains.
Here’s what you should look for:
Recommendations that are tailored to your site, industry, and goals
Clear explanations of why certain actions matter now
An SEO roadmap tailored to your goals and what you must expect at different stages
Here’s a list of what to ask and how you can evaluate their responses:
“How do you decide what to work on first?”: An SEO expert explains priorities and tradeoffs, and not just a checklist of tasks. Agencies usually start with an SEO audit that can help them identify quick wins before moving onto bigger optimization tasks.
“What would you prioritize in the first few months, and why?” Be wary of vague responses or promises that sound identical across industries.
4. Pay attention to how they communicate
How an SEO expert communicates during the sales process is often how they’ll communicate after you hire them.
Good SEO partners explain things in plain language, answer questions directly, and adjust their explanations based on your level of experience. They’re also comfortable slowing down when something isn’t clear.
In addition, good SEO agencies will be transparent with you when they don’t have all the answers.
5. Evaluate reporting before you ever see a report
You need to understand how an agency thinks about reporting and SEO success. Reliable SEO experts talk about reporting as a tool for decision-making, not just a record of activity.
Sarah advises that the SEO expert you’re evaluating must have case studies to back up their understanding of your industry and SEO in general. “Do those case studies align with your language for success? Remember, your north star metric is qualified leads, not traffic.”
If reporting is framed as something that’s delivered rather than discussed, it often becomes a formality instead of a planning tool.
When an SEO expert explains their reporting approach, listen for whether they:
Talk through how they review performance, not just what they report
Look at other metrics beyond traffic
Explain how trends or changes influence priorities
Describe specific actions they take based on results
Reference ongoing discussion, not one-way reporting
6. Ask how they handle processes and change
This is an important one. Successful SEO strategies have robust systems in place to ride the waves of change in SEO and your industry.
SEO is always evolving. And your business may pivot strategies to adapt to industry shifts like AI Overviews and algorithm updates.
Professional SEO agencies must be able to adapt to trends throughout your partnership and still deliver results.

What is Upwork?
Upwork is a site that allows freelancers to apply to jobs set up by businesses who need help.
As one of those potential businesses, that means you’d create a job and open it up to freelancers to apply. After they do, you look at your prospects and choose the one you think would do the best job for your business.
Once the work is done, you pay the freelancer, and Upwork takes a cut from their compensation.
Upwork was actually created from the marriage of two freelance bidding sites, Elance and oDesk, in 2015. Both companies had reached their levels of possibility, and merged to create a larger entity.
Because Elance and oDesk shared many of the same freelancers, they didn’t necessarily double in size, but they could service more businesses from one location.
For many companies, the idea of hiring contractual workers from Upwork to perform SEO projects seems to make fiscal sense.
The company is able to set its price, and to choose from — hopefully — dozens of potential freelance SEO writers and marketers. In turn, it gets cheap SEO that leaves the budget unscathed.
However, as with most opportunities that seem too good to be true, this type of arrangement is less than ideal.

The problem with hiring SEO freelancers from Upwork
There is a huge problem innate with hiring SEO workers from Upwork, and it has absolutely nothing to do with Upwork itself or the money you pay.
Instead, it has to do with something much less tangible — maintenance.
SEO constantly changes, and using freelancers provides you with a cheap, short-term solution. But having one person work on SEO for your entire site won’t stand the test of time. You’ll have to change it eventually, and when you do, are you going to shell out for another freelancer?
On top of that, SEO is not a one-man (or one-woman) job.
One freelancer could potentially write keyword-rich copy for your blog, but the freelancer from Upwork likely has no direction without you going to another freelancer who can help you develop keywords.
And even if the freelance writer claims to be able to help you develop keywords and write copy, who is going to do your SEO analytics? Who will make sure the SEO is actually working? And who is going to be able to help you when you want to make rapid changes on your website, with your SEO campaign or on your social media?
Last — and most importantly — SEO takes a long time to take effect. So you could pay a freelancer a few hundred dollars today to get a couple quick fixes to your site, but you won’t know if they work for at least three weeks after they’re implemented.
In other words, you could pay a lot of money to have someone optimize your site, pay them, and then their work doesn’t pay off.
Now, you’re hung out to dry with no one who can step in to tell you what’s wrong or how to fix it. The freelancers aren’t under contract — they worked for a one-time payment, and once it’s paid, they don’t have any obligation to your business anymore.
And that’s not to say that Upwork’s freelancers don’t know what they’re doing — they could know their stuff inside and out.
The problem is that SEO is a multi-faceted tool that’s best handled by a team. The more people who are working on the SEO together for the sole purpose of helping your company, the more value you will get for every cent you put into your company’s SEO campaigns.
