What to take from WebFX and Mediagarh, and where MMC leapfrogs them.

WebFX shows how a content and SEO machine is built at scale. Mediagarh shows how an Indian agency owns a vertical. Neither has built for how India actually buys. This page breaks both down across content, design, development and SEO, and turns each finding into a move for MMC’s new site.

The short version

  • Architecture is WebFX’s moat133 service pages, 41 pricing pages, 400+ industry URLs, discipline hubs, glossaries, statistics pages and tools, all funnelling into one proposal form on every page. MMC should build the folder structure on day one, even if many folders start with a single page.
  • One template, executed everywhere52 of WebFX’s 70 search and paid service pages share one section order. That is how they publish at volume. MMC should copy the discipline of a single strong template, and write unique copy for each service so pages don’t read as clones.
  • Mediagarh owns a verticalEvery title ends “for educational institutions”, the vocabulary is admissions and enrolments, testimonials come from chairmen and trustees, and admission season drives urgency. That playbook is exactly what MMC’s education landing page should run, with more depth.
  • The India layer is emptyAcross 460 WebFX pages, ₹, COD, RTO, UPI, GST and “contribution margin” never appear. Mediagarh has no Indian admissions calendar, no tools and no CRM-to-ads loop. Pages built around Indian D2C economics and Indian admissions operations have no real competition.
  • Conversion is a system, not a buttonWebFX runs a one-field website capture on every page and scores calculator use as leads in the background. Mediagarh’s paid landing page uses a three-field form and a sticky enquiry tab. MMC should combine an audit, calendar booking, WhatsApp and calculators that hand off to a person.
  • Speed and stack are a real edgeWebFX carries 31 third-party services and 1.7 MB of JavaScript yet shows its main content in about 1.5 s. Mediagarh’s server is fast, but service pages take over 4 s to show their headline. MMC’s build compiles React in the visitor’s browser and its homepage weighs 13.7 MB. Fixing that makes MMC the fastest of the three.

Lens 1

Content: what they say and how they say it

WebFXA revenue story, told on thousands of pages

  • The pitch: US full-service retainers plus a proprietary platform (RevenueCloudFX, about 15 modules) and the Nutshell CRM they own. Around 100 services sit in the menu, from SEO to Walmart ads and AI agents.
  • The enemy: “agencies that report clicks”. A guide titled “My marketing agency isn’t driving results” catches buyers who are unhappy elsewhere.
  • Headline formulas: “Move From X to Y”, “Turn Your X Into a Y”, a big number as the H1, a year stamp (“Start Ranking in 2026”). Nearly every call to action is a possessive “Get My Free Proposal”.
  • Definition-first openers on newer pages give search engines and chat assistants a sentence to quote.

Our approach

The service page, in order

  1. Stats carousel and outcome H1 (“X Services That [outcome]”), with a single “Enter your website” field
  2. “What do our X services include?” in tabs
  3. Platform pitch (RevenueCloudFX)
  4. Pricing preview: “Pricing custom to you. Starting at $X”
  5. Client stories and testimonials
  6. Comparison grid: WebFX vs in-house vs a typical agency
  7. FAQs, a full plans table with deliverables, reviews, six related-service links

Median length is about 1,870 words. FAQs lean towards definitions and cost: of 446 questions on the search and paid pages, 98 are “What is X?” and 54 are about price, while only 6 cover account ownership, contracts or minimums. That leaves the serious buyer’s questions for MMC to own.

Pricing as content

Case studies and content engine

  • Case study format: header facts (industry, company size, services), headline, three stat tiles, a quote, before and after bullets, a three-step approach, the named team with hours dedicated, then a budget form. About 820 words, filterable by industry and service.

  • Tools: calculators with no signup; name and email are asked only in exchange for a PDF, report or guide.

MediagarhCategory language, from headline to case study

  • The pitch: full-stack marketing for education only, from a Jodhpur team of 44 plus two founders. SEO, Google and Meta ads, web, video, creative, print, PR and lead automation.

  • A dozen CTAs on one page

Lens 2

Design: the visual systems

WebFXDisciplined, dense, SaaS-poster

  • Type: Inter for everything, headlines at weight 800 to 900 with tight tracking, one word per headline recoloured blue.
  • Colour: white and pale blue-grey grounds, action blue #207DE9 on every primary button, near-black headings, grey body copy.
  • Imagery: real staff photography, cut-out headshots in coloured circles, dashboard mock-ups with floating stat bubbles, a circular “Revenue Engine” diagram, partner badge carousels.
  • Rhythm: hero, stat strip, alternating two-column blocks, card carousels, CTA band, repeated. Pages run about 10,500 px on desktop and 15,800 px on mobile.
  • Navigation: five mega-menus with about 100 links, plus a business-challenge column (“My website doesn’t convert”). Industries and locations sit in the footer.
  • Components: carousels everywhere, tabbed “what’s included”, three-column comparison tables, plans tables, sticky header, “To top” tab.

MediagarhCinematic hero, four styles underneath

  • Hero: a full-bleed night sky with a lone figure on a dune, white ALL-CAPS Satoshi. Memorable, but it says nothing about campuses or students.
  • Below it: white pages, two-tone headings in charcoal and steel blue (#3889C9), a dense five-column logo wall of about 45 education brands, count-up stat bands.
  • Four visual styles: the cinematic homepage, a flat corporate paid landing page with a line-art campus, dark neon case pages, and tabloid-style social creatives.
  • Type: nine font families load (Poppins, Averta, Montserrat, Roboto, Satoshi, Lato, Questrial, Albert Sans, Rubik). Mobile body text is 13.7 px.
  • Video: looping background video in service heroes, nine testimonial videos per service page.

On a phone

Design moves for MMC

  • Keep MMC’s system (royal blue, sun yellow, Raleway, the Cormorant italic accent). It already looks more considered than either rival; extend it to service, industry, pricing and tool templates.
  • Replace stock-style art with real work: ad creatives, landing pages, dashboards in rupees, before and after screens.
  • Give the education page campus and people photography, not a night sky, so it doesn’t echo Mediagarh.
  • Borrow WebFX’s component set (tabs for inclusions, plans tables, comparison tables) but keep pages shorter and calmer.
  • On mobile: a slim cookie bar, a bottom WhatsApp and call bar, and the form within the first two screens.

What stands out

  • WebFX treats the site as a sales system. Visitor identification, lead scoring, A/B testing and speed monitoring run on every page. Their slower server response is offset by caching and lazy loading.
  • Mediagarh shows the Elementor trade-off. The server is quick, but headlines inside animated or video heroes appear after 4 seconds, no images are lazy-loaded, and nine font families load.
  • MMC’s build is the lightest in requests but the heaviest in images, and compiling JSX in the browser adds a large script download and processing on every visit.

Development moves for MMC

  • Pre-compile the JSX at build time and self-host React; drop in-browser Babel.
  • Convert images to WebP or AVIF with responsive sizes and explicit width and height; target under 1 MB before the fold.
  • Fix the pre-render so animated counters bake their final value, and fix the one page with a large layout shift.
  • Serve from an edge CDN with long cache headers and HTTP/3, as WebFX does.
  • Generate pages, llms.txt, sitemaps and structured data from one content data file.
  • Add the pieces WebFX proves out, lean: tool usage as lead events, CRM stages sent back to Google and Meta, simple A/B testing and real-user speed monitoring.
  • Add a skip link and keep body text at 16 px or above on mobile.
