# 05 Competitor Landscape: productised AI services (agencies and tools)

Research date: 2026-09-28. Method: WebSearch plus WebFetch of homepages and service pages. Prices and claims are recorded as stated by the source or by third-party roundups. Nothing here is fact-checked (per standing instruction). Where a page was JS-rendered or thin, this is flagged as "not observed" rather than guessed.

Caveat: the 3minds page itself (3mindsdigital.com/AI-by-3minds) returned only its title through WebFetch (likely client-rendered), so it could not be used as a baseline here. Compare against the 3minds teardown in the sibling research files.

## A. Agencies with a productised AI-services page or hub

### 1. DigiChefs / ChefsAI (India, Mumbai) - closest structural match to 3minds
- URL: https://digichefs.com/chefsai-ai-marketing-solutions/
- Sells: a named AI suite: AI Creative Studio (graphics, motion), AI Films, VogueAI Studios (AI model photoshoots), PersonaLift (automated talking-head videos), AI Product Photoshoot and UGC, AI Website builder, AI Agentic (chatbots/customer agents).
- Packaging/price: not shown; "GET A QUOTE" led.
- Positioning: "Creativity meets machine precision"; "serving AI-powered brilliance on a digital platter" (culinary metaphor runs through the copy).
- Site presentation: hero with Get a Quote, service cards, testimonials carousel, FAQ on AI adoption, repeated "Let's Talk More" CTAs, a sticky corner chatbot ("Deep", styled as a co-founder), a Google Slides "See AI Work" link as the demo (weak), case studies sorted by service, industry vertical showcase (pharma, real estate, fashion).
- Tactics: each AI product gets a proprietary brand name (ChefsAI, VogueAI, PersonaLift). The sub-brand naming is the strongest idea; the demo layer is the weakest (slides, not embedded video).

### 2. Sqroot (India) - AI UGC as a priced agency product
- URL: https://sqroot.in/services/ugc-ads-services/
- Sells: AI UGC ad videos with AI avatars (male/female, ethnicities, accents; Hindi, Hinglish and other Indian languages). Bundle: creative strategy, script from product details, avatar pick, voice and lip-sync, branding, 9:16 / 1:1 / 16:9 delivery.
- Price as stated: from Rs 60,000, tied to monthly ad spend up to Rs 6 lakh. Compares to Rs 10,000-25,000 per creator video; claims 1/10th cost and 48-hour turnaround.
- Positioning: "Engineered by AI, Enriched by Human Creativity."
- Presentation: "BOOK A STRATEGY CALL" buttons with urgency ("September Slots are Filling Fast"), multiple lead forms, D2C founder testimonials, ROAS case-study metrics.
- Tactics: price tied to ad spend (performance-agency logic); hybrid AI+human line; urgency slots.

### 3. Trigital Solutions (India)
- URL: https://trigitalsolutions.com/services/ai-video-production/
- Sells: AI video for marketing, product demos, UGC, training, social.
- Price as stated: from Rs 10,000; "up to 80% savings".
- Presentation: standard service-page format (not deeply observed). Notable for a public "starts at" price, which is rare among agencies.

### 4. Vigyapan Waale (India: Mumbai, Delhi, Bangalore, Dubai)

## F. Floating / revolving AI badge or button, site-wide

Findings are thin. No agency was found in this pass that runs a distinctive revolving or rotating "AI" badge as a brand device.

What exists:
- DigiChefs: sticky corner chatbot styled as a person ("Deep, co-founder"). This is the only agency-side floating AI element observed. It is a chat bubble, not a badge to a services hub.
- Generic widget market (Elfsight AI Chatbot widget: floating bubble, floating input, embedded window; Bubble "Floating AI Assistant" plugin: fixed corner button, custom avatar, typing dots, typewriter effect; Discovered Labs AI Assist Widget: floating bar with buttons for ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Grok, Gemini). These prove the pattern is a commodity widget: a corner bubble that opens chat.
- SaaS tools (Arcads, HeyGen, Wati) rely on sticky top-nav CTAs rather than floating badges (from the fetched summaries; floating elements may exist but were not visible in text extraction).

Implication: a floating AI button that opens a services menu (photoshoot, video, UGC, sales agent, landing page) rather than only a chat window is differentiated. Revolving text ring or orbiting badge on that button is not something competitors were seen to do. Treat the 3minds button as the reference.

## Analysis

### Crowded versus open
- Very crowded: AI UGC/avatar video tools (Arcads, Creatify, MakeUGC, HeyGen, Synthesia, Captions, Koro); AI product photography tools (Pebblely, Photoroom, Flair, Claid, Koro); AI landing page builders (Unbounce, Instapage, Framer, Durable); WhatsApp/chat agents (Wati, AiSensy, Interakt, Haptik, Yellow.ai, Gupshup, Intercom Fin, Tidio). Do not try to out-tool these; MMC cannot win on self-serve price.
- Moderately crowded: Indian agencies selling AI UGC or AI ad films (Sqroot, Trigital, Vigyapan Waale, NYX Studio, DigiChefs). Mostly single-service pages or quote-led; only DigiChefs has a multi-product hub.
- Open: a done-for-you AI menu bundled with a performance-marketing engine, packaged per segment (D2C, B2B, education) with clear entry pricing and live demos. Tools sell software; agencies mostly sell one service or hide price. Almost nobody pairs AI production with distribution, testing and reporting as one productised offer.
- Open: education-specific AI (admissions AI agent on WhatsApp, AI spokesperson/faculty explainer videos, course landing pages). Wati has an eLearning vertical; no Indian agency was found positioned on AI-for-education-marketing.
- Open: B2B-specific AI (AI SDR/sales agent tied to lead gen, AI founder-led video, AI landing pages per campaign). Tools are generic; agencies are D2C-skewed.

### Unclaimed positioning for MMC
1. "AI production plus performance" as one line: creative that ships into ads, is tested, and reported. Competitors sell outputs (Koro, Arcads) or ad management (agencies) separately. Sqroot gets closest by tying price to ad spend.
2. Segment fluency: one AI menu with three entry points (D2C, B2B, education). Koro claims India-for-D2C only at tool level; nobody claims education/B2B.
3. Hybrid human accountability at agency scale: "AI speed, strategist accountability" (Sqroot's "Enriched by Human Creativity" is the nearest; avoid copying the phrasing).
4. Hinglish/Indian-language and India-market realism (festivals, COD, marketplace formats) is claimed by Koro as a tool; MMC can claim it as a service with named strategists.
5. Transparent starting prices. Trigital, Sqroot, Vigyapan Waale show it works in SEO snippets and qualifies leads; DigiChefs and most others hide it.

### What MMC should copy
- Sub-brand naming per AI product (DigiChefs: VogueAI, PersonaLift) to make services memorable and searchable.
- Work-first hero (Superside): show outputs immediately, benefits second.
- Before/after and template galleries (Pebblely, Koro) as visual proof; matches MMC's rule that every text block needs a literal scene.
- Three-step flow copy (Koro: add product, pick actor, get video) for each service page.
- One dominant CTA repeated (Arcads) plus a low-friction alternative; slot-scarcity urgency (Sqroot) used honestly.
- Use-case and vertical pages for SEO (HeyGen, Wati verticals, Pebblely marketplace pages, Koro India-long-tail blog). Create "AI photoshoot for [category] in India" and "AI UGC for [education/B2B/D2C vertical]" pages.
- Comparison/pricing content (Creatify vs Arcads pattern): "AI photoshoot vs studio shoot cost India" pages.
- Sticky corner element (DigiChefs) but as a menu launcher, not only a chatbot.
- Embedded playable demos, not slide links (DigiChefs weakness).

### How to differentiate
- Floating AI button that opens a mini-menu of the services with a live tiny demo per item, then routes to the right segment page. Keep the hover-to-project pattern MMC already approved for consistency.
- Lead with D2C but show B2B and education tiles with equal visual weight (never D2C-only).
- Package as menus: entry (single asset pack), monthly (creative plus ad testing), and full-funnel (AI + media). Show "from" prices in INR for entry packs, quote for the rest.
- Tie AI outputs to a measurable performance loop (creative testing cadence, reporting), which tools cannot offer.
- Say "a few of the brands we have worked with"; do not state client counts.
- Avoid claiming numbers (ROAS, views) that competitors use as stats blocks unless MMC owns real proof; competitors' stat-block style (Wati, Arcads, Koro) is heavily used and reads as generic.

### Design, development, SEO observations for leverage
- Competitor sites present AI mostly as image grids and testimonial carousels; few use autoplay muted video loops in the hero for each service. Video-first service cards would look richer than the tool sites' static galleries.
- Interaction patterns seen: template galleries, drag-and-drop canvas (Flair), text-to-video flow (HeyGen), sign-in-with-Google (Arcads). An agency can mimic these as "try a sample" widgets that capture a lead (upload a product photo, get a preview by email).
- SEO: competitors win on three patterns: city/country modifiers ("AI ad film maker India"), competitor-comparison pages, and per-use-case pages. Indian agencies mostly use the first only.
- Gaps: many agency pages lack FAQ schema, pricing schema and video schema; adding them would help both classic and AI-answer visibility.
