Chapter 01

When we got the call.

October 2024. DeoBar's founder called us on a Friday. By Monday, the deck on their board was due, and the projection wasn't going to clear.

The brand had crossed ₹4Cr in the previous 12 months and was doing the right shapes on the surface: a 4.2× ROAS, monthly creative refreshes, three agencies running specialized stacks. But blended margin was at 6% and slipping. They were hitting the wall most D2C brands hit between ₹4Cr and ₹8Cr, and they didn't know why.

"I've spent ₹2.4Cr on Meta in 12 months and I cannot tell you which 200k of it actually built a customer versus rented attention I never owned."

Aanya Sharma · Founder, DeoBar

We did a 5-day audit. The findings, in order of severity:

  1. D60 retention was 28%. Their category average is 52%. Every new customer was bleeding before LTV could pay back CAC.
  2. Meta had 87 active ad sets and 312 creatives with no naming convention and overlapping audiences. The algorithm was cannibalizing itself.
  3. The website was 4.2s LCP on mobile. Add-to-cart was a 3-step modal. A 14% conversion improvement was sitting in plain sight.
  4. Klaviyo had 6 flows that hadn't been touched in 11 months. Welcome series sent the same email to a first-time buyer and a sixth-purchase loyalist.
  5. The brand voice across creative was inconsistent: three agencies producing three different tones, none of them the founder's.
Chapter 02

The 90-day plan.

We refused the project at first. The founder wanted us to "fix Meta." That isn't a fix. We came back two days later with a single-page proposal: rebuild the system end-to-end, one pod, 90 days, retention-first. They signed it that afternoon.

Days 1–14

Foundation

  • Brand voice rewrite (3 voice pillars, 30 example lines)
  • Meta account rebuild, 3-tier funnel
  • Server-side CAPI + cohort-tagged events
  • Klaviyo flows audit + first 3 rebuilt
  • Site speed: 4.2s → 1.8s LCP
Days 15–45

Production

  • 220 new creatives shipped (60 UGC, 100 static, 60 motion)
  • All 14 Klaviyo flows rewritten
  • Welcome → Second purchase email arc launched
  • WhatsApp lifecycle layer added
  • Subscription mechanic introduced
Days 46–90

Compound

  • Creative iteration on winners, 40 new units/wk
  • Spend scaled from ₹18L/mo to ₹84L/mo
  • Loyalty mechanic, points + tiers - went live
  • First subscription cohort hit 81% D90 retention
  • Brand campaign launched across all touchpoints
Chapter 03

What the math did.

The first week of December, six weeks in - was when the numbers turned. Cohort CAC dropped 41%. Blended ROAS crossed 8× for the first time. By the end of Q1, we were running ₹84L/mo in Meta spend at an 11.8× blended ROAS, a number we didn't promise and would have called optimistic.

Chapter 04

What we'd do differently.

  1. We waited too long on subscription.

    The subscription mechanic was the single highest-leverage move and we shipped it in week 6. We should have done it in week 1. Future retainers, D2C - get subscription scoped into the first sprint.

  2. We over-tested early.

    Forty creative concepts running simultaneously means Meta can't learn. Now we constrain to 6–8 in week one, find winners, then scale variation.

  3. Klaviyo deserved its own week.

    We tried to redo flows in parallel with Meta. They deserved a dedicated sprint. Now every retainer has Week 3 reserved for retention infrastructure.
