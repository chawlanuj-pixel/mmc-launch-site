Two ways to shorten the burn

Costs nothingMonth 9

Ten more points of repeat rate pulls break-even in from month 10 and cuts the dip to ₹16.17L. Repeat orders cost nothing to acquire.

Counterintuitive₹20.31L

Growing 5 points slower shrinks the cash you need from ₹18.22L to this: fewer customers bought above break-even each month.

The real target₹670

Break-even CAC. Every rupee you take off today's ₹900 shortens the burn from the other end.

Paying ₹900 against a break-even CAC of ₹670 means losing money on every new customer, normal for a new brand. Marketing is charged only on new customers here, because repeat orders cost nothing to acquire. That's why repeat rate shortens the burn from both ends at once.

Growing faster usually costs more, not less

More new customers each month at above-break-even CAC deepens the dip even though it pulls the profitable month closer. At 12% growth you need ₹18.22L; at 7% you'd need ₹20.31L. The question isn't how fast you can grow, it's how much of the dip you can fund.

Questions this calculator answers

How long does a new D2C brand take to reach break-even CAC?

It depends on how far above break-even you start and how fast the account learns. A brand paying 900 rupees against a 670 rupee break-even CAC, improving 6% a month, typically crosses into monthly profit somewhere between month 8 and month 14, but the number that matters is the cumulative cash burned getting there, not the month itself.

How much cash does a new D2C brand need before it turns profitable?

Total burn equals the deepest point of your cumulative cash curve. Every month of losses added together before the first profitable month. It is almost always larger than founders expect, because growing faster increases the trough before it shortens it.

Does growing faster shorten the burn?

Not usually. Faster growth means more new customers acquired above break-even CAC each month, which deepens the cash trough even though it brings the profitable month closer. Repeat rate is the lever that shortens the burn from both ends, because repeat orders cost nothing to acquire.
